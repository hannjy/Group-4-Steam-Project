2026-09-06

We chose to work on the Steam Games Dataset 2025. From this, we selected the games_march2025_cleaned subset to explore and use for our projects.

The dataset was sourced from Kaggle, where the data was created with Steam store page scraping and Steam Games Scraper repository, which gathers data using the Steam API and Steam Spy. It consists of all the games available on the digital gaming distribution platform steam in March of 2025, and provides extensive information about each individual game.

  This was of interest for us because we thought that the game data could help to build informative analysis that tells a story and shows insightful correlations. We are able to conduct both broad and specific analysis which gives us a-lot of material to work with for answering specific questions.

It consists of 89,618 rows, one per Steam application identified by appid, and is cross-sectional as each game is observed once at the March 2025 collection date. The variables are mixed: continuous and discrete numerical measures such as price, pct_pos_total and count variables like dlc_count and peak_ccu; binary (windows, mac, linux); ordinal bins (estimated_owners); nominal fields stored as lists (publishers, genres, categories, supported_languages); and unstructured text.

Explicit missingness is minimal, under 1% for every retained variable, the largest being categories at 0.9775% empty and publishers at 0.6104%. However, missingness is instead encoded through sentinel values which will be addressed during EDA.

We only used the games_march2025_cleaned subset of the Steam Games Dataset 2025, and did not include any of the data from the other subsets.

Figure 3: Number of games released on Steam per year, 1997-2025


The number of games released on Steam has increased significantly, from 1,525 titles in 2014 to 18,282 in 2024, with 2024 alone representing a 43.6% increase on the previous year. The trend is not entirely monotonic, with a decline of 17.4% in 2019. Notably, 67.6% of all games in the dataset were released from 2020 onwards, meaning the catalogue is heavily weighted towards recent titles. This growth is consistent with the lowering of barriers to publication on the platform and the increasing accessibility of game development tools, although the dataset alone cannot distinguish between such explanations. Note that the 2025 figure is low, as the data were collected in March 2025 and therefore do not capture the full year.
